Seven rented machines in one night, and what a memory controller's busy fraction predicts

A decode step on Qwen3-8B keeps one card's memory controller busy 87 % of the time; on gemma-4-26B-A4B, 38 %. Read that number on one card and it says which of the two gains more from a faster card, from a second card, from faster memory, and loses more to slower memory — on hardware it was never measured on. It does not say by how much, and a prediction committed before the data says so.

Abstract

Every projection in this repository was built from two Radeons under a desk and whatever Colab offered. This is what $29.94 of rented time added: six machine configurations on the decode and prefill ladder — H100, H200, B300, RTX PRO 6000, two H100s, two PRO 6000s — eight on the collective, 586 ladder measurements and 385 collective cells with 0 errors, and the first context past 32 000 anywhere here. Two platform controls come first: an A100 and an L4 rented on the same platform reproduce Colab's committed rows to 0.07 % and 0.9 %, which is what lets any of the ratios below be read as a card difference.

The finding is one counter. campaign-2026-09-02d found that what a second Radeon is worth is decided by mem_busy, the memory controller's busy fraction on one card — 90 % and 1.70× on the bf16 8B, 56 % and 1.20× on the w4a16 12B. That was five cells on one box. It now orders the answer in five settings — one on the pair and four against a single H100 baseline: an H200, a B300, a PRO 6000 — the other direction, because its memory is slower — and a second H100 over NVLink, which is worth 2.9 % to the model that reads least. It does not predict magnitude. A prediction for the H200 was committed before that run: the ordering came back right at both ends and the spread came back nearly double, with the memory-bound end exceeding the memory-clock ratio itself, which the form used cannot produce. Ordinal, not cardinal, and on the record as such.

The wire is not the axis, and now that is measured. Across eight configurations the collective's bandwidth end spans 62× and its latency end 3.2×; batch-1 decode reduces one row and lands on the latency end. NVLink is worth 20 % at two cards and 171 % at four. Three more things fell out: four cards chose three attention backends with no flag set; a $7.10/h B300 loses to a $3.95/h H100 on the MoE and wins by 66 % on the 8B; and past 32 000 what flattens a decode curve is a bounded attention window, not a recurrent state — which falsified a prediction written into the runner before the run.

1Two controls, before any ratio

Every number below divides a card rented on Modal by an A100 or an L4 that Colab measured in August. That gap between platforms was an assumption until it was measured, so the same two cards were rented on the same platform and run on the same models at the context budget and ladder Colab used.

2One counter orders five settings

mem_busy_pct is the fraction of samples in which the card's memory controller was busy, read by the same sampler on every machine (harness/telemetry.py: amdgpu's mem_busy_percent on the Radeons, NVML's utilization.memory on CUDA). It is a busy fraction, not an achieved bandwidth — the harness records achieved bandwidth as absent on both platforms and says why — and on 2026-09-02 it was read on one Radeon, on two models, and found to decide what the second card was worth. 3And sizes none of them

An ordering across five settings is a correlation until it predicts something. An H200 is an H100 with the same compute capability, the same 700 W cap, the same SM clock ceiling and faster memory, so it is the cleanest test the counter could be given: if a decode step spends a fraction f of its time waiting on memory and the rest on everything else, then multiplying memory speed by r gives 1 / ((1 − f) + f / r). f was taken as mem_busy on the H100 and r as the two cards' measured maximum memory clocks — 2 619 against 3 201 MHz, so 1.222; the vendors' bandwidth figures were not used because this repository did not measure them. The six predictions were committed before the run.

4Why the second card's value is not on the wire

The a100 article's section 9 used to say the second Radeon's bandwidth was spent on the interconnect. allreduce-2026-09-02 withdrew that with a measurement on the pair: 16.6–21.5 µs per collective under graph replay, about a tenth of a step, paid equally by a model the second card helps 1.70× and one it helps 1.20×. The same sweep on seven NVIDIA configurations gives the withdrawal a mechanism.

5Four cards, three attention backends, nobody asked for any of them

One vLLM, one command line, no backend flag anywhere in the sweep — and the runner reads the backend out of every serve log rather than trusting a table, which is the only reason the following is known. vLLM 0.28 has no flag for the decoder's backend at all (attention_backend exists only in config/speculative.py, read out of the installed package), so the control that would separate the card from the kernel is not available.

6The newest card is not the fastest card

7Past 32 000: what makes a curve flat

Every ladder in this repository stopped at 32 000 tokens until this night; the H100 carried five models to 128 000. A prediction was written into the runner before the run: gemma-4 reads a KV cache that grows without bound, Qwen3.8-27B carries a recurrent state that does not grow at all, and Muse-Glimmer-30B attends through a 2 048-token window — a term that grows and then stops — so it should land between the two.

8What is not established

The counter's magnitude

Section 3 is the whole of it: the form 1 / ((1 − f) + f / r) with f read off one card under-predicts the spread by half and cannot produce the memory-bound end at all. Either the effective bandwidth ratio exceeds the memory-clock ratio — the vendors' figures give 1.43, at which the same form over-predicts every row — or the H200 differs from the H100 in something the prediction assumed away. Nothing here separates those.

A backend control

The B300's column in Figure 6 and every gemma-4 comparison across the four cards carry a backend as well as a card, and vLLM 0.28 offers no way to hold the decoder's backend still. Not measured.

Two configurations, by arithmetic and by budget

gemma-4-26B-A4B at TP=4 cannot run: CompressedTensors WNA16 MoE with static group scales requires the MoE intermediate size per tensor-parallel partition to be divisible by group_size (32), a property of the checkpoint and not a failure to retry. The other four models at TP=4, and Muse-Glimmer at TP=2 on the rented pairs, were not bought.

Achieved bandwidth, and one counter on two vendors

mem_busy is a busy fraction. 87 % at one absolute rate is not 87 % at another, and the harness records achieved bandwidth as absent on both platforms. The counter travels; what it is a fraction of does not. It is also two counters: amdgpu's mem_busy_percent on the Radeons and NVML's utilization.memory on the CUDA cards, read by one sampler but defined by two drivers. That the same two models land at 90/56 on one and 87/53 on the other is evidence they measure alike, not proof.

Four of the five settings share a baseline

The H200, B300, PRO 6000 and second-H100 rows all divide by the same H100 sitting, so an error in that sitting would move four rows together. The two platform controls bound that sitting against Colab to 0.07 %, and the pair's row rests on a different box entirely; the five are one independent check on the pair's finding, not five.

Where the dropped prefill rungs sit

Twenty-three prefill cells on the rented cards failed the repeatability cut, and every one of them is at 500 to 2 000 tokens — the cold engine's first requests. Two decode cells failed it too, both on the H200: gemma-4-26B-A4B at 1 000 and gemma-4-31B at 500. Nothing above rests on any of them.
