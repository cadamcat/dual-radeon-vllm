A second RX 7900 XT buys 1.1× at decode
and 1.9× at prefill

Decode rate and prefill scaling on dual Radeons; checked against a single card, an A100 and an L4

Adding a second consumer card to a batch-1 decode buys 1.11× to 1.23× and no more. At prefill the same card cuts a 32 K prompt from 40.1 s to 21.5 s — 1.87×. Both measurements ran on the same two cards on the same day. What changed is the job they were asked to do.

Abstract

This article used to ask a single question — two consumer cards against one A100 — and that question bundles three separate issues. Measuring each card on its own pulls them apart: what one consumer card is worth against one datacentre card, what a second consumer card adds, and only then what the pair delivers.

The second card is nearly free of effect at decode. Across eleven depths and two models it buys 1.11× to 1.23×. The per-step all-reduce has since been timed, and it is not the bottleneck: it takes roughly a ninth of a step. On the same machine, Qwen3-8B reaches 1.70× from its second card while paying the same collective. What holds these two models back is not the interconnect; section 9 says so rather than guessing. At prefill the same card buys 1.56× to 1.87× in wall time, because attention parallelises with no communication at all.

Prefill is two terms and they do not move together. Fitting T(S) = a + bS + cS², against one 7900 XT the A100 leads by 3.3× on b and 6.7× on c for the dense 12B. The gap in attention scaling is twice the gap in compute. On the mixture-of-experts, where 4B active parameters shrink the compute term, the two gaps match at 5.75× and 5.71×. Datacentre silicon is not uniformly faster either: the L4 is slower than a consumer 7900 XT on b (0.90×) and 3.0× better on c. Datacentre versus consumer is not a single axis.

Evaluating the pair against one A100 is the third step, returning to where this article began. Without speculation, the A100 leads by 1.30× to 1.46× across all eleven depths of the ladder. That margin is flat throughout, and nothing like the U that four single-run points suggested.

1What is being compared

Four machines enter the comparison: one RX 7900 XT, two of them at TP=2, one A100-SXM4-80GB and one L4. Not every model runs on every machine across the three architectures; two gaps stem from arithmetic rather than omissions. gemma-4-26B-A4B stops at 12 000 tokens on one 7900 XT: holding 16.96 GiB of weights on a 19.98 GiB card leaves only 0.93 GiB for KV cache even at 0.95 utilisation, whereas 32 K would need about 0.97. And gemma-4-31B-it has no single-card row at all — 22 GB of weights do not fit into a 20 GB card, so the pair is the only way this machine runs it. The 31B enters only at the third step and appears nowhere before it.

Where decode and prefill come from Wherever possible, the first three steps take decode and prefill numbers from the same session. The comparison claims that the second card behaves differently across the two jobs; mixing sessions would inject cross-run variance into that claim. That requirement anchors the A100 on 2026-08-30 rather than 2026-08-29: the earlier session has decode, but 0 of 11 rungs reached chart grade at prefill because prefix caching was active. Since every rung is a strict prefix of the next, caching billed repeated tokens for nothing.

This consistency is a preference rather than an absolute rule. Figure 3 draws the 2026-08-29 campaign for both platforms because only that sitting measured two speculative arms against one another. Figure 4's A100 prefill data reflects a later re-measurement by construction, as the first pass produced none that survives. Each side of each comparison is one run; when two sides date from different days, the figure marks the difference. The two A100 sessions agree to 0.4 % on this model past 2 K, and to 5.5 % at the two shallowest rungs.

The 31B step retains its original configuration: checkpoint gemma-4-31B-it-qat-w4a16-ct, batch 1, and greedy sampling. Both platforms run on the TRITON_ATTN attention backend. Heterogeneous head dimensions in this model force Triton whenever FA4 is unavailable, which holds true for gfx1100 and for an A100 alike.

Each side is on its healthy path, and that took a patch Both columns route speculation through the 3D flash-decoding path. Stock vLLM disables that path under speculation on either platform, as documented in a separate article. Comparing stock configurations directly would merely compare two instances of the same defect. The fix ports vllm#45450's mechanism identically to both stacks.

Prefill is fitted, not quoted. A single prefill tok/s number states nothing because prompt scaling is non-linear: time to first token over S tokens follows T(S) = a + bS + cS². The two primary machines differ by three times on one term and nearly seven on the other. Below, fits come from `build_prefill.fits()`—the same ones prefill.jsonl --fits reports—over chart-grade rungs only. The constant a is fitted but never quoted: it drifted from 9.8 ms to 99.8 ms across two campaigns on the same arm, so this ladder does not measure it.

2One card against one card

Begin without tensor parallelism: one 7900 XT, one A100, and one L4, evaluated across the same ladder on two models. The A100 leads throughout, by a margin that varies by model and contracts with depth. The L4 does not lead at all.

The L4 is a datacentre card that loses to a consumer card at this job. Across both models and every context depth, it reaches only 0.54× to 0.68× of one 7900 XT. What it exhibits instead is flatness: it retains 89 % of its 500-token decode rate at 32 K, whereas the A100 keeps 62 %. That stability is a property of a small card with slow memory, not an advantage. The takeaway from this comparison is that datacentre is not a tier, and the next figure is where the L4 earns its place.

3What the second card buys

The second 7900 XT forms the flattest line in Figure 1. Across eleven depths on the dense model it buys 1.13× to 1.23×, and across seven on the mixture-of-experts 1.11× to 1.12×. Scaling is not even monotone: on the 12B the ratio peaks at 1.23× at 6 000 tokens before dropping to 1.13× at 32 K.

A batch-1 decode step is bandwidth-bound and brief. Splitting model weights halves the bytes read per GPU, which is the whole gain. Every step then concludes with an all-reduce whose latency does not shrink with the split. This page used to conclude that what the second card contributes in bandwidth it largely spends on the wire. Timed, the wire is about a ninth of the step — 1.85 ms of 16.70 on the 12B — and a model on this same box gets 1.70× from its second card while paying the same collective. The collective latency is measurable, but it is not the limit; see section 9.

Prefill is the opposite case, and it is the same two cards. Attention over a prompt parallelises across heads without communication, and prompts are long enough that per-step overhead becomes a rounding error. Fitting both arms and recombining terms at the deepest rung each pair reached: 40.1 s to 21.5 s at 32 K on the dense model, 1.87×; and 6.21 s to 3.99 s at 12 K on the mixture-of-experts, 1.56×. Same hardware, same day, four to seven times the gain.

4Prefill is two terms, and they do not move together

The linear term b represents GEMM throughput—how fast the machine multiplies. The quadratic term c measures how severely attention scales with prompt length. A prefill tok/s metric is simply a weighted average of the two at a single prompt length, shifting with every prompt depth. Three readings emerge, and none survives being averaged into one number:

On the dense model the A100's attention advantage is twice its compute advantage — 6.67× against 3.29×. Whatever limits the 7900 XT at prefill, it is not primarily the multiplier.

On the mixture-of-experts the two collapse together, 5.71× against 5.75×. The model activates 4B of its 26B parameters per token, so the GEMM term stays small on both machines and the ratio between them ceases to reflect matrix multiplication.

The L4 is slower than a consumer card on b and three times better on c. 0.90× and 3.01×. That explains why it is 1.58× faster than a 7900 XT on a 32 K prompt yet loses on a short one: the two curves cross, making prefill advantage entirely a question of prompt length. The second card, for comparison, buys 1.44–1.48× on b and 1.91–2.22× on c — improving the quadratic term more than the linear term, mirroring the wall-clock findings above.

5Two cards against one A100

The gap is flat at about 1.4×. Running stock software without patches on either side, the A100 leads 1.36× at 500 tokens and 1.44× at 32K — narrowest at the shallowest depth, widest at the deepest, across a sixty-four-fold range of context. That answers the question this article's title used to ask, matching the comparison published in the repository README.

The lead is what speculation costs the A100. Speculation must be evaluated from the 2026-08-29 pair, because it is the only session that ran both arms on both machines — and its Radeon side runs three patches the A100 side does not, so its level matters less than its difference. Without speculation that session puts the A100 ahead 1.30× to 1.46×, close to the stock ladder above it. Switch MTP on and the lead vanishes: 0.88× at 500 tokens, level from 2K through 8K, and only 1.08× at 32K. The Radeons do not get faster. The A100 gives up more by speculating — and not because it drafts badly. Its acceptance is the better of the two, 1.92–2.07 tokens a step against 1.46–1.53. It loses because a speculative step costs it 2.40–2.57× a plain decode step, whereas the same step costs the Radeons 1.35–1.39×. At batch 1 the pair has spare compute inside each step; the single card lacks headroom, making the fixed price of drafting and verifying four tokens a much larger tax on its work. Both figures reside in benchmarks/campaign-2026-08-29/ and benchmarks/cuda-a100/campaign-2026-08-29/.

The U was four points. Enable the probe and the curve appears — 1.48× at 1K, down to 1.14× at 16K, back to 1.87× at 32K — an anomaly this article was originally named for. Eleven matched rungs on the same two machines, averaged over two rounds a cell, produce a flat 1.36× to 1.44× instead. The 16K point that looked like a minimum was a single cell of one run, whose deepest neighbor compared two different depths. The shape did not survive replication; the raw points remain visible on the page because retracting data quietly is worse than acknowledging it openly.

And now at prefill too. The A100 side of this comparison lacked usable prefill until 2026-08-30: the session that measured its decode ran with prefix caching on, across a ladder whose every rung prefixes the next, charging repeated tokens for nothing and failing repeatability criteria. Re-taken with caching off across the five models running on both systems, prefill reveals dynamics invisible in decode:

The compute term does not care which kernel is on the path, and the attention term is almost nothing else. Across all five models the A100 leads by 1.92× to 4.00× on b, with overlapping ranges between kernel implementations. On c they diverge completely: 2.99× to 3.10× on the three models the A100 serves through the same Triton unified-attention family the Radeons use, and 11.94× and 19.01× on the two it serves through FlashAttention.

Five models differ in more than their kernel, so that is a correlation. Here is the same effect inside one machine, one day and one model. Qwen3.8-27B on these cards is routed to ROCM_ATTN by default and can be pinned to TRITON_ATTN with a flag. Pinned, its c is 17.27 ns/token² and the A100 is 11.94× ahead. Left alone, its c is 4.46 and the A100 is 3.08× ahead. One flag, no patch, and the answer to "how far ahead is the A100 on long prompts" moves by 3.87×. That flag was termed free by this repository until 2026-08-30; it buys 1.15× of decode at 32 K and sells this.

None of which diminishes the A100: it remains ahead on every model and both terms. What it does mean is that a single number for “how much faster is the datacentre card” is not a property of the two cards: on the quadratic term it is mostly a property of which attention kernel each side ended up on, and on this hardware that is decided by a routing table and one command-line flag.

6The split is what costs, not the silicon

The 2D attention path—which speculation falls into under stock vLLM—launches one workgroup per KV head. This model has 16. On one card a rank sees all of them; split across two, each rank sees eight. The starved path is starved twice as hard by parallelism, visible directly in retention:

7And it erodes speculation too

The MTP drafter is four layers deep, making the extra forward pass cheap. What is not cheap is that every draft step remains a step. Under TP=2, each step incurs the baseline all-reduce floor regardless of the model evaluated — measured at 2.30 ms on the 31B, invariant to drafter depth. Speculative decoding is therefore less advantageous on two cards than on one.

9What is not established

Batched throughput, and prefill's constant term

Benchmarks run at batch 1. Batched throughput is a different regime and is not measured at all; where decode stops being bandwidth-bound, the second card's 1.1× has no reason to hold, and neither does anything else on this page. Prefill is measured now, but only its two shape terms: the constant a is fitted and never quoted, because on one arm it moved from 9.8 ms to 99.8 ms between two campaigns a month apart and on another it came out negative. A short enough prompt is therefore outside what these fits describe.

gemma-4-31B on one card

The 31B has no single-card row and cannot have one on this box: 22 GB of weights, 20 GB of card. The first two steps rely on the other models; the 31B enters only at the third step where dual-card execution is required.

The A100's own derived bandwidth

Figure 7 exists for Radeons with no counterpart on the other platform. The 1.27× marker in Figure 3 is a ratio of datasheet figures, an upper bound on what the bandwidth difference alone can explain, not a prediction either side was measured against.

One stack per side, and one card of each kind

Two vLLM versions run native stacks across environments, including rented Colab VMs with unquantified variance. Only one A100 and one L4 were measured once: nothing separates card architecture from instance effects.

The speculative pair is not patch-matched

Figure 3's solid line carries the comparison using stock code on both sides. The 2026-08-29 pair is not patch-matched: its Radeon arms run vllm#45916, the window block-skip and vllm#45450, while unspeculative A100 arms omit all three and speculative arms run only vllm#45450. With no unpatched run covering both arms, MTP cost must be read off relative differences rather than absolute levels. Speculative configuration matches: serve logs record SpeculativeConfig(method='mtp', …, num_spec_tokens=3) against the identical assistant checkpoint, asserted by verify_doc_figures.py.

Why the second card behaves as it does

This page used to say that fixed all-reduce latency ate most of the second card's bandwidth during batch-1 decode, and that no all-reduce had been timed to check it. One has been now, and it does not support that reading. Timed under HIP-graph replay—logged as cudagraph_mode FULL_AND_PIECEWISE and Capturing CUDA graphs (decode, FULL)—an all-reduce at the batch-1 shape costs 16.6 to 21.5 µs. Across 2 × layers + 1 collectives (tallied on 2026-09-03: two per layer plus embedding), communication takes 1.85 ms of the 12B's 16.70 ms step, about a ninth.

And the same box gives Qwen3-8B 1.70× from its second card at decode while giving the 12B 1.185×, on collectives that cost within 0.6 ms of each other per step. Were the wire eating bandwidth, the 8B could not reach 1.70×. What holds the 12B back is not the wire. Subtracting perfect halving and the measured collectives leaves 4.96 ms of its step unexplained; the same subtraction on the 8B leaves 0.66 ms. (Answered 2026-09-02: the premise was flawed.) The model assumed memory bottlenecking; yet on one card the 8B keeps the memory controller 90 % busy versus 56 % on the 12B. Across five cells, scaling mirrors that counter alone (campaign-2026-09-02d). The 12B's 4.96 ms is not missing time; it is the null model being wrong for a controller half idle. That counter has since ordered the worth of faster memory, a second card and a slower card across four rented NVIDIA configurations, which is its own article.

The half of the old sentence about attention still stands: attention parallelises with no communication at all, and the prefill numbers above are what show it. The three claims pricing collectives from fitted intercepts remain withdrawn: they were unmeasurable on this ladder, replaced by direct timings 49 to 63 times smaller. The measurement is in the repository, with the runtime library identified from /proc/self/maps rather than a version constant.
