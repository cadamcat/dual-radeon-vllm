The second card buys 1.1× at decode
and 1.9× at prefill

One 7900 XT, two of them, one A100 and one L4, on the same ladder

Adding a second consumer card to a batch-1 decode is worth 1.11× to 1.23× and no more. On prefill the same second card cuts a 32 K prompt from 40.1 s to 21.5 s — 1.87×. These are the same two cards on the same day; what changed is which of the two things you asked them to do.

Abstract

This article used to ask one question — two consumer cards against one A100 — and that question bundles three. This round measured each card on its own, so they come apart: what one consumer card is worth against one datacentre card, what a second consumer card then buys, and only then what the pair is worth.

The second card is nearly free of effect at decode. Across eleven depths and two models it buys 1.11× to 1.23×. The all-reduce it pays on every step has since been timed and is not the reason: it is about a ninth of a step, and the same box gives Qwen3-8B 1.70× from its second card while paying the same collective. What holds these two models back is not the wire, and section 9 says so rather than guessing. At prefill the same card buys 1.56× to 1.87× of wall time, because attention parallelises with no communication at all.

Prefill is two terms and they do not move together. Fitting T(S) = a + bS + cS², against one 7900 XT the A100 is 3.3× on b and 6.7× on c for the dense 12B: the gap in attention scaling is twice the gap in compute. On the mixture-of-experts, whose 4B active parameters shrink the compute term, the two gaps are the same — 5.75× and 5.71×. And the L4, a datacentre card, is slower than a consumer 7900 XT on b (0.90×) and 3.0× better on c. "Datacentre against consumer" is not one axis.

The pair against one A100 is then the third step, and it is where this article started. Without speculation the A100 leads by 1.30× to 1.46× at every depth of an eleven-rung ladder — flat, and nothing like the U four single-run points suggested.

1What is being compared

Four machines: one RX 7900 XT, two of them at TP=2, one A100-SXM4-80GB and one L4. Three models, and not every model on every machine, because two of the gaps are arithmetic rather than omissions. gemma-4-26B-A4B stops at 12 000 tokens on one 7900 XT: 16.96 GiB of weights resident on a 19.98 GiB card leaves 0.93 GiB of KV even at utilisation 0.95, and 32 K would need about 0.97. And gemma-4-31B-it has no single-card row at all — 22 GB of weights do not go into a 20 GB card, so the pair is the only way this box runs it. The 31B is therefore the model of the third step and of nothing before it.

Where decode and prefill come from The first three steps take both numbers from the same run wherever one exists, because the claim they make is that the second card is worth something different at the two jobs and reading those off different sessions would put a session boundary inside the comparison. That is what fixes the A100 on 2026-08-30 rather than 2026-08-29 there: the earlier session has decode, but its prefill is 0 of 11 rungs chart-grade — prefix caching was on and every rung of this ladder is a strict prefix of the next, so a repeat was charged for nothing.

It is a preference and not a rule. Figure 3 is the 2026-08-29 campaign on both sides because that is the one that measured two speculative arms against each other, and Figure 4's A100 prefill is a later re-measurement by construction — the first pass produced none that survives. Each side of each comparison is one run; where the two sides are different days, the figure says so. The two A100 sessions agree to 0.4 % on this model past 2 K, and to 5.5 % at the two shallowest rungs.

The 31B step keeps the configuration it always had: the same checkpoint, gemma-4-31B-it-qat-w4a16-ct, batch 1, greedy, and on both platforms the attention backend is TRITON_ATTN — this model's heterogeneous head dimensions force it whenever FA4 is unavailable, which is true of gfx1100 and of an A100 alike.

Each side is on its healthy path, and that took a patch Both columns run speculation through the 3D flash-decoding path, which stock vLLM refuses under speculation on either platform — the subject of a separate article. Comparing the stock configurations instead would be comparing two instances of the same bug. The mechanism is vllm#45450's, ported identically to both stacks.

Prefill is fitted, not quoted. A single prefill tok/s number states nothing: the time to first token over a prompt of S tokens goes as T(S) = a + bS + cS², and the two machines this article compares differ by three times on one term and nearly seven on the other. The fits below are `build_prefill.fits()`'s, the same ones prefill.jsonl --fits reports, over chart-grade rungs only. The constant a is fitted but never quoted: it moved from 9.8 ms to 99.8 ms across two campaigns of the same arm, so this ladder does not measure it.

2One card against one card

Start with no tensor parallelism anywhere: one 7900 XT, one A100, one L4, the same ladder, the same two models. The A100 leads, by a factor that depends on the model and shrinks with depth. The L4 does not lead at all.

The L4 is a datacentre card that loses to a consumer card at this job. It runs at 0.54× to 0.68× of one 7900 XT across both models and every depth. What it has instead is flatness — it retains 89 % of its 500-token rate at 32 K where the A100 retains 62 % — and that is a property of a small card with slow memory, not an advantage. The line worth taking from this figure is that "datacentre" is not a tier, and the next figure is where the L4 earns its place.

3What the second card buys

The second 7900 XT is the flattest line in Figure 1. Across eleven depths on the dense model it buys 1.13× to 1.23×, and across seven on the mixture-of-experts 1.11× to 1.12×. It is not even monotone: on the 12B it rises to 1.23× at 6 000 tokens before falling to 1.13× at 32 K.

A batch-1 decode step is both bandwidth-bound and short: splitting the weights halves the bytes each card reads, which is the whole gain, and every step then ends in an all-reduce whose cost does not shrink with the split. This page used to conclude that what the second card contributes in bandwidth it largely spends on the wire. Timed, the wire is about a ninth of the step — 1.85 ms of 16.70 on the 12B — and a model on this same box gets 1.70× from its second card while paying the same collective. So the collective is real and it is not the limit; see section 9.

Prefill is the opposite case, and it is the same two cards. Attention over a prompt parallelises across heads with no communication at all, and the prompt is long enough that the per-step floor is a rounding error. Fitting both arms and putting the two terms back together at the deepest rung each pair reached: 40.1 s to 21.5 s at 32 K on the dense model, 1.87×; and 6.21 s to 3.99 s at 12 K on the mixture-of-experts, 1.56×. Same hardware, same day, four to seven times the gain.

4Prefill is two terms, and they do not move together

The linear term b is GEMM throughput — how fast the machine multiplies. The quadratic term c is how badly attention scales with prompt length. A prefill tok/s number is a weighted average of the two at one prompt length, and the weighting changes with every prompt. Three readings, and none of them survives being averaged into one number:

On the dense model the A100's attention advantage is twice its compute advantage — 6.67× against 3.29×. Whatever is limiting the 7900 XT at prefill, it is not mainly the multiplier.

On the mixture-of-experts the two collapse together, 5.71× against 5.75×. The model activates 4B of its 26B parameters per token, so the GEMM term is small on both machines and the ratio between them stops being about the GEMMs at all.

The L4 is slower than a consumer card on b and three times better on c. 0.90× and 3.01×. That is why it is 1.58× faster than a 7900 XT on a 32 K prompt and would lose on a short one: the two curves cross, and which card is "faster at prefill" is a question about prompt length. The second card, for comparison, buys 1.44–1.48× on b and 1.91–2.22× on c — more of the quadratic term than the linear one, which is the same fact the wall-clock numbers above state.

5Two cards against one A100

The gap is flat at about 1.4×. Stock on both sides, with no patch on either, the A100 leads 1.36× at 500 tokens and 1.44× at 32K — narrowest at the shallowest rung, widest at the deepest, across a sixty-four-fold range of context. That is the whole answer to the question this article's title used to ask, and it is the same comparison the repository README publishes.

The lead is what speculation costs the A100. Speculation has to be read off the 2026-08-29 pair, because it is the only session that ran both arms on both machines — and its Radeon side runs three patches the A100 side does not, so its level matters less than its difference. Without speculation that session puts the A100 ahead 1.30× to 1.46×, close to the stock ladder above it. Switch MTP on and the lead is gone: 0.88× at 500 tokens, level from 2K through 8K, and only 1.08× at 32K. The Radeons do not get faster. The A100 gives up more by speculating — and not because it drafts badly. Its acceptance is the better of the two, 1.92–2.07 tokens a step against 1.46–1.53. It loses because a speculative step costs it 2.40–2.57× a plain decode step, where the same step costs the Radeons 1.35–1.39×. At batch 1 the pair has compute to spare inside a step; the single card does not, so the fixed price of drafting and verifying four tokens is a much larger fraction of what it was already doing. Both figures are in benchmarks/campaign-2026-08-29/ and benchmarks/cuda-a100/campaign-2026-08-29/.

The U was four points. Turn the probe on and it is there — 1.48× at 1K, down to 1.14× at 16K, back to 1.87× at 32K — and this article was named for it. Eleven matched rungs of the same two machines, two rounds a cell, produce a flat 1.36× to 1.44× instead. The 16K point that looked like a minimum is one cell of one run, and its deepest neighbour compares two different depths. The shape did not survive being measured again; the numbers behind it are still on the page because retracting a figure quietly is worse than showing what it said.

And now at prefill too. The A100 side of this comparison had no usable prefill until 2026-08-30: the session that measured its decode ran with prefix caching on, over a ladder whose every rung is a prefix of the next, so a repeat was charged for nothing and none of it survived the repeatability cut. Re-taken with the cache off, for the five models the pair also runs, it says something the decode half does not:

The compute term does not care which kernel is on the path, and the attention term is almost nothing else. Across all five models the A100 is 1.92× to 4.00× ahead on b, and the two kernel groups' ranges overlap. On c they do not come near each other: 2.99× to 3.10× on the three models the A100 serves through the same Triton unified-attention family the Radeons are on, and 11.94× and 19.01× on the two it serves through FlashAttention.

Five models differ in more than their kernel, so that is a correlation. Here is the same thing inside one machine, one day and one model. Qwen3.8-27B on these cards is routed to ROCM_ATTN by default and can be pinned to TRITON_ATTN with a flag. Pinned, its c is 17.27 ns/token² and the A100 is 11.94× ahead. Left alone, its c is 4.46 and the A100 is 3.08× ahead. One flag, no patch, and the answer to "how far ahead is the A100 on long prompts" moves by 3.87×. That flag is the one this repository called free until 2026-08-30; it buys 1.15× of decode at 32 K and sells this.

None of which makes the A100 slower than it is. It is ahead on every model and both terms. What it does mean is that a single number for "how much faster is the datacentre card" is not a property of the two cards: on the quadratic term it is mostly a property of which attention kernel each side ended up on, and on this hardware that is decided by a routing table and one command-line flag.

6The split is what costs, not the silicon

The 2D attention path — the one speculation falls into on stock vLLM — launches one workgroup per KV head. This model has 16. On one card a rank sees all of them; split across two, each rank sees eight. The starved path is therefore starved twice as hard by the parallelism, and it shows up as retention:

7And it erodes speculation too

The MTP drafter is four layers deep, so the extra forward pass is cheap. What is not cheap is that every draft step is still a step, and a step under TP=2 pays the all-reduce floor whatever the model attached to it costs — 2.30 ms on the 31B, measured, and it does not shrink with the drafter. So the arithmetic that makes speculation worth having is worse on two cards than on one.

9What is not established

Batched throughput, and prefill's constant term

Everything here is batch 1. Batched throughput is a different regime and is not measured at all; where decode stops being bandwidth-bound the second card's 1.1× has no reason to hold, and neither does anything else on this page. Prefill is measured now, but only its two shape terms: the constant a is fitted and never quoted, because on one arm it moved from 9.8 ms to 99.8 ms between two campaigns a month apart and on another it came out negative. A short enough prompt is therefore outside what these fits describe.

gemma-4-31B on one card

The 31B has no single-card row and cannot have one on this box — 22 GB of weights, 20 GB of card. So the first two steps are carried by the other two models, and the 31B appears only from the third step on, where the pair is the only way this machine runs it at all.

The A100's own derived bandwidth

Figure 7 exists for the Radeons and has no counterpart. The 1.27× reference in Figure 3 is a ratio of datasheet numbers, so a reader should treat it as an upper bound on what the bandwidth difference alone can explain, not as a prediction either side was measured against.

One stack per side, and one card of each kind

Two different vLLM versions, because each side runs the stack it actually uses; two different operating environments, two of them rented Colab VMs whose cross-session variance is unquantified. And there is one A100 and one L4, each measured once: nothing here separates a card from its instance.

The speculative pair is not patch-matched

Figure 3's solid line is stock on both sides with no patch on either, which is why it carries the comparison. The 2026-08-29 pair drawn under it is not: its Radeon arms run vllm#45916, the window block-skip and vllm#45450, and the A100 arms run none of those without speculation and only vllm#45450 with it. No unpatched session ran both speculation arms, so what MTP costs each machine has to be read off that pair or not at all — the difference between its two arms is what is claimed, not the level either arm sits at. The speculation itself does match: both serve logs record SpeculativeConfig(method='mtp', …, num_spec_tokens=3) against the same assistant checkpoint, and verify_doc_figures.py asserts it from the logs.

Why the second card behaves as it does

This page used to say that a batch-1 decode step is short enough for a fixed all-reduce to eat most of a second card's bandwidth, and that no all-reduce had been timed to check it. One has been now, and it does not support the reading. Under HIP-graph replay — the mode a decode step actually runs in, which the serve logs record as cudagraph_mode FULL_AND_PIECEWISE and Capturing CUDA graphs (decode, FULL) — one all-reduce at the batch-1 shape costs 16.6 to 21.5 µs on this pair. Over 2 × layers + 1 collectives (counted on 2026-09-03: two per layer and the embedding's one) that is 1.85 ms of the 12B's 16.70 ms step, about a ninth of it.

And the same box gives Qwen3-8B 1.70× from its second card at decode while giving the 12B 1.185×, on collectives that cost within 0.6 ms of each other per step. If a fixed all-reduce were eating most of what the second card contributes, the 8B could not be getting 1.70×. What holds the 12B back is not the wire. Subtracting perfect halving and the measured collectives leaves 4.96 ms of its step unexplained; the same subtraction on the 8B leaves 0.66 ms. (Answered 2026-09-02, and the premise was the problem.) The subtraction assumed a step that was waiting on bytes; on one card the 8B keeps the memory controller 90 % busy and the 12B 56 %, and across five cells the second card's gain follows that counter and nothing else in the row (campaign-2026-09-02d). The 12B's 4.96 ms is not a cost gone missing; it is the null model being wrong for a model whose memory controller is half idle. The same counter has since ordered the worth of faster memory, a second card and a slower card on four rented NVIDIA configurations, which is its own article.

The half of the old sentence about attention still stands: attention parallelises with no communication at all, and that is what the prefill numbers above show. The three claims that tried to price the collective from a fitted intercept remain withdrawn — they were withdrawn for being unmeasurable from this ladder, and the number that replaces them is 49 to 63 times smaller. The measurement is in the repository, with the library it ran through identified from /proc/self/maps rather than from a version constant.
