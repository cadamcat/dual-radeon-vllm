How to measure decode on a machine like this

Two harnesses in this repository produce decode figures, and charts that pool them need the difference to be a number rather than an assumption. It is 0.44 % — once the machine is warm. Finding that out took four identical runs, the first of which read 31 % low.

__CHIPS__

Abstract

The campaign harness drives an OpenAI server, streams, samples at temperature 0.8, and reports over a 512-token generation. The probes drive LLM.generate offline, greedy, and difference a 64-token run against an 8-token one. On matched depths they agree to 0.44 %, 0.01 % and 0.07 %, and matching the generation span too changes nothing that matters. They are the same measurement.

Getting there required discovering that a single run is not a measurement on this box. Four identical runs back to back read −30.7 %, −5.1 %, and then converge; the deficit is largest exactly where the machine is fastest, which is why it first looked like a harness difference rather than a warm-up effect.

Two rules fall out and are encoded in the ledger rather than left to whoever draws the chart. Every point carries runs and range_pct; and patches is a list, not a boolean, because a point that needs an unmerged pull request is not the same claim as one you get by installing a release.

The range is reported rather than a standard deviation, and one cell shows why. Adding a third and fourth run to a bimodal 8K point moved its range from 15.79 % to 16.79 % — wider — while its standard deviation fell from 4.87 to 3.61. One statistic said the cell had become less trustworthy and the other said it had become more.

1Do the two harnesses measure the same thing

The calibration model is chosen to be immune to every other variable in play, which is the whole reason it is that model:

    Engine knobs are the campaign's, and the depths are the campaign's own reported prompt_tokens rather than the nominal targets, so the two sides sit at the same context instead of merely near it.

    2The three runs that cost, and what they bought

    The first attempt at Figure 1 showed the probe 31 % below the campaign at the shortest depth and 9 % below at the longest — a difference with a plausible-sounding story attached, since the harnesses really do differ in span, sampling and transport. It was none of those.

    Why this does not retroactively damage the campaigns Each campaign cell starts a fresh container whose engine initialisation — model load, compile, graph capture — takes minutes before anything is timed, and the campaigns already run two rounds per cell which agree to 0.2 %. The arm-order-reversed pair in a separate experiment agrees to 0.3 % at 1K and 0.2 % at 32K, which a first-cell penalty of this size would have broken. The rule that follows is for probe runs starting from a cold machine: discard the first rather than average it in.

    3The control that spans five weeks

    Two campaigns, five weeks apart, different kernel, different patches — and six configurations measured in both. Those six are the only thing that says whether a number from July and a number from August can be put on one chart.

    4What every point carries, and where the line is

    The ledger is a projection of the raw files onto one row format, and it is regenerated and compared rather than edited. Two things travel with every point because leaving them to the chart is how a repository ends up promising more than it measured.

    5Why a range and not a standard deviation

    Almost every cell here has two runs. At two, the sample standard deviation is the range divided by the square root of two — the same number wearing a distribution it has not earned. At four it starts to mean something, and that is where the argument gets concrete, because one cell has four.

    6Why output comparison is not the correctness test here

    The obvious way to check that a kernel change is harmless is to compare generated tokens before and after. On this machine that measures the machine rather than the change.

    So numerical claims in this repository are made against an fp32 reference on fixed inputs, and end-to-end changes are argued from rates and from routing recorded inside the workers. Comparing token ids is used only where the platform is known to be deterministic — which, measured, is the A100 and not this box.

    7What is not established

    Why the first run is slow

    The effect is measured and its shape is described; the mechanism is not. It survives a warm-up generation, it is not confined to the first depth, and it converges by the third run. Whether it is the host, the driver, the container or something else was not isolated.

    Whether the harnesses agree on other models

    The calibration is one model, chosen precisely because no other variable in this repository can touch it. That makes the comparison clean and makes it narrow: a model on a different kernel path could differ between the two harnesses for reasons this calibration says nothing about.

    The two controls that missed

    Two of six configurations moved by about 0.8 % between campaigns, and one of those is the subject of its own investigation that has ruled out several candidates without finding the cause. Cross-campaign pooling is therefore stated with a band, not asserted as equivalence.

    What varies in the greedy output

    No claim is made that any of the varying outputs is wrong, only that they are not reproducible. TP=1 is untested, and the source is above the kernel but not further localised.

    8What has changed since

    2026-08-26 · the first calibration attempt was the interesting one It compared one probe run against the campaign and produced a 31 % harness difference with a plausible explanation available. Three more runs turned that into a warm-up effect. The rule this article exists to state — that a single run is not a measurement here — comes from nearly publishing the opposite.
    2026-08-29 · the cut's justification was restated build_ledger.py justified its 6 % threshold by describing the spread distribution as it stood when the rule was written. That distribution moved: the 0.27 rows landed and the bimodal cell gained two runs, so the tail is now 2.6, 3.4, 3.5, 4.1, 5.1 and then 16.8, and the cut excludes one point rather than two. The comment now describes the current distribution and the verifier pins its tail, so it cannot go stale unnoticed again.
