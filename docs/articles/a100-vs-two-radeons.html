Two consumer RX 7900 XTs
against one A100

The gap is U-shaped, and both ends are about the split

On batch-1 decode of the same 31B model, on each platform's own healthy path, the A100 is 1.48× ahead at 1K of context, 1.14× ahead at 16K, and 1.87× ahead at 32K. The gap is U-shaped, the two ends are consistent with different costs, and neither of them is the nominal bandwidth ratio.

2× RX 7900 XT · gfx1100 · TP=2 one A100-SXM4-80GB · TP=1 gemma-4-31B w4a16 · MTP on measured 2026-08-26

Abstract

Batch-1 decode is bandwidth-bound, and the nominal ceilings are only 1.27× apart: two cards at 800 GB/s against one at 2.0 TB/s. The measured advantage sits below that at 8K and 16K and above it at both ends.

At the short end the tax is tensor parallelism. The step time is small enough that TP=2's fixed all-reduce floor is a visible fraction of it, and the single card pays nothing for something it does not do. At the long end the KV scan dominates and realized bandwidth compounds; the Radeons reach 63 % of their 800 GB/s on this model.

Two further readings come out of the same table, and both are about tensor parallelism rather than about either vendor. The starved attention path starves harder when split: its 2D launch grid is one workgroup per KV head, so two cards see 8 each against the single card's 16, and retention from 1K to 30–32K is 15.8 % against 33.6 %. And speculation's economics erode: every draft step pays the same all-reduce floor, so the net win over no speculation is +7.5 % here against +39.2 % there.

What this does not cover is where the A100 wins outright. Prefill and batched throughput are compute-bound, and there the comparison is against RDNA3 WMMA that already realizes about 37 % of nominal peak. None of that is measured here; if it is compute-bound, the expectation is multiples rather than percentages.

1What is being compared

The same checkpoint, gemma-4-31B-it-qat-w4a16-ct, with the same official MTP assistant at num_speculative_tokens: 1, batch 1, greedy, decode isolated by differencing a 64-token generation against an 8-token one. On both platforms the attention backend is TRITON_ATTN — this model's heterogeneous head dimensions force it whenever FA4 is unavailable, which is true of gfx1100 and of an A100 alike.

Each side is on its healthy path, and that took a patch Both columns run speculation through the 3D flash-decoding path, which stock vLLM refuses under speculation on either platform — the subject of a separate article. Comparing the stock configurations instead would be comparing two instances of the same bug. The mechanism is vllm#45450's, ported identically to both stacks.

One run per cell on both sides. These are probes, not campaigns: single processes, no within-cell samples. The Radeons' rungs are powers of two and the A100's longest leg is 30 000 tokens, so that pair is the only one not matched exactly, and the figure says which. Third-digit differences here are not separated from noise.

2The gap, and the shape of it

The lead is what speculation costs the A100. Both lines above have MTP on, because that is the configuration the RDNA3 write-up arrived at. Measured again on 2026-08-29 with both arms and eleven matched rungs, the picture separates. Without speculation the A100 is ahead by 1.30× to 1.46× at every depth, flat and unremarkable. Switch MTP on and that lead is gone: 0.88× at 500 tokens, level from 2K through 8K, and only 1.08× at 32K. The Radeons do not get faster. The A100 gives up more by speculating — and not because it drafts badly. Its acceptance is the better of the two, 1.92–2.07 tokens a step against 1.46–1.53. It loses because a speculative step costs it 2.40–2.57× a plain decode step, where the same step costs the Radeons 1.35–1.39×. At batch 1 the pair has compute to spare inside a step; the single card does not, so the fixed price of drafting and verifying four tokens is a much larger fraction of what it was already doing. Both figures are in benchmarks/campaign-2026-08-29/ and benchmarks/cuda-a100/campaign-2026-08-29/.

The A100 being fourteen per cent ahead at 16K is the number worth sitting with. It is not a claim that the hardware is equivalent — it is a claim about one regime: single-stream decode, one model, mid-length context, each side on its best available path. Move to prefill or to batch and the same two machines are not close.

3The split is what costs, not the silicon

The 2D attention path — the one speculation falls into on stock vLLM — launches one workgroup per KV head. This model has 16. On one card a rank sees all of them; split across two, each rank sees eight. The starved path is therefore starved twice as hard by the parallelism, and it shows up as retention:

4And it erodes speculation too

The MTP drafter is four layers deep, so the extra forward pass is cheap. What is not cheap is that every draft step is still a step, and a step under TP=2 pays the all-reduce floor whatever the model attached to it costs. The floor does not shrink with the drafter, so the arithmetic that makes speculation worth having is worse on two cards than on one.

6What is not established

Anything outside single-stream decode

Prefill and batched throughput are compute-bound and are not measured here at all. On this hardware prefill saturates at about 37 % of nominal FP16 peak, and the A100's tensor cores are a different class of thing. The honest expectation there is multiples rather than percentages, and it is an expectation, not a measurement.

The A100's own realized bandwidth

Figure 4 exists for the Radeons and has no counterpart. The 1.27× reference in Figure 1 is a ratio of datasheet numbers, so a reader should treat it as an upper bound on what the bandwidth difference alone can explain, not as a prediction either side was measured against.

One model, one stack per side, one run per cell

Two different vLLM versions, because each side runs the stack it actually uses; two different operating environments, one of them a rented Colab VM whose cross-session variance is unquantified. The campaign-grade version of this comparison has not been run.

Why 16K is the minimum

The U is described, not explained at its bottom. That the short end is dominated by a fixed per-step cost and the long end by the KV scan is consistent with every measurement here, but the crossing itself was not located, and only four depths were measured.
