One 7900 XT, two of them, one A100 and one L4, on the same ladder

Adding a second consumer card to a batch-1 decode is worth 1.11× to 1.23× and no more. On prefill the same second card cuts a 32 K prompt from 40.1 s to 21.5 s — 1.87×. These are the same two cards on the same day; what changed is which of the two things you asked them to do.

Abstract

This article used to ask one question — two consumer cards against one A100 — and that question bundles three. This round measured each card on its own, so they come apart: what one consumer card is worth against one datacentre card, what a second consumer card then buys, and only then what the pair is worth.

The second card is nearly free of effect at decode. Across eleven depths and two models it buys 1.11× to 1.23×. At batch 1 a decode step is bandwidth-bound and short, and TP=2 pays a fixed all-reduce on every one of them, so most of the second card's bandwidth is spent talking to the first. At prefill the same card buys 1.56× to 1.87× of wall time, because attention parallelises with no communication at all.

Prefill is two terms and they do not move together. Fitting T(S) = a + bS + cS², against one 7900 XT the A100 is 3.3× on b and 6.7× on c for the dense 12B: the gap in attention scaling is twice the gap in compute. On the mixture-of-experts, whose 4B active parameters shrink the compute term, the two gaps are the same — 5.75× and 5.71×. And the L4, a datacentre card, is slower than a consumer 7900 XT on b (0.90×) and 3.0× better on c. "Datacentre against consumer" is not one axis.

The pair against one A100 is then the third step, and it is where this article started. Without speculation the A100 leads by 1.30× to 1.46× at every depth of an eleven-rung ladder — flat, and nothing like the U four single-run points suggested.

1What is being compared

Four machines: one RX 7900 XT, two of them at TP=2, one A100-SXM4-80GB and one L4. Three models, and not every model on every machine, because two of the gaps are arithmetic rather than omissions. gemma-4-26B-A4B stops at 12 000 tokens on one 7900 XT: 16.96 GiB of weights resident on a 19.98 GiB card leaves 0.93 GiB of KV even at utilisation 0.95, and 32 K would need about 0.97. And gemma-4-31B-it has no single-card row at all — 22 GB of weights do not go into a 20 GB card, so the pair is the only way this box runs it. The 31B is therefore the model of the third step and of nothing before it.

Decode and prefill for a machine come from one session This article's central claim is that the second card is worth something different at prefill than at decode, so reading the two off different runs would put a session boundary inside the comparison. Every arm below therefore takes both numbers from the same run. That is what fixes the A100 on 2026-08-30 rather than 2026-08-29: the earlier session has decode, but its prefill is 0 of 11 rungs chart-grade — prefix caching was on and every rung of this ladder is a strict prefix of the next, so a repeat was charged for nothing. The front page picks per figure instead and draws A100 decode from the earlier session; on the mixture-of-experts the two sessions differ by 2.2 %.

The 31B step keeps the configuration it always had: the same checkpoint, gemma-4-31B-it-qat-w4a16-ct, batch 1, greedy, and on both platforms the attention backend is TRITON_ATTN — this model's heterogeneous head dimensions force it whenever FA4 is unavailable, which is true of gfx1100 and of an A100 alike.

Each side is on its healthy path, and that took a patch Both columns run speculation through the 3D flash-decoding path, which stock vLLM refuses under speculation on either platform — the subject of a separate article. Comparing the stock configurations instead would be comparing two instances of the same bug. The mechanism is vllm#45450's, ported identically to both stacks.

Prefill is fitted, not quoted. A single prefill tok/s number states nothing: the time to first token over a prompt of S tokens goes as T(S) = a + bS + cS², and the two machines this article compares differ by three times on one term and nearly seven on the other. The fits below are `build_prefill.fits()`'s, the same ones prefill.jsonl --fits reports, over chart-grade rungs only. The constant a is fitted but never quoted: it moved from 9.8 ms to 99.8 ms across two campaigns of the same arm, so this ladder does not measure it.

2One card against one card

Start with no tensor parallelism anywhere: one 7900 XT, one A100, one L4, the same ladder, the same two models. The A100 leads, by a factor that depends on the model and shrinks with depth. The L4 does not lead at all.

The L4 is a datacentre card that loses to a consumer card at this job. It runs at 0.54× to 0.68× of one 7900 XT across both models and every depth. What it has instead is flatness — it retains 89 % of its 500-token rate at 32 K where the A100 retains 62 % — and that is a property of a small card with slow memory, not an advantage. The line worth taking from this figure is that "datacentre" is not a tier, and the next figure is where the L4 earns its place.

3What the second card buys

The second 7900 XT is the flattest line in Figure 1. Across eleven depths on the dense model it buys 1.13× to 1.23×, and across seven on the mixture-of-experts 1.11× to 1.12×. It is not even monotone: on the 12B it rises to 1.23× at 6 000 tokens before falling to 1.13× at 32 K.

The reason is that a batch-1 decode step is both bandwidth-bound and short. Splitting the weights halves the bytes each card reads, which is the whole gain; but every step then ends in an all-reduce whose cost does not shrink with the split, and at batch 1 there is only one token's worth of work to amortise it over. What the second card contributes in bandwidth it largely spends on the wire.

Prefill is the opposite case, and it is the same two cards. Attention over a prompt parallelises across heads with no communication at all, and the prompt is long enough that the per-step floor is a rounding error. Fitting both arms and putting the two terms back together at the deepest rung each pair reached: 40.1 s to 21.5 s at 32 K on the dense model, 1.87×; and 6.21 s to 3.99 s at 12 K on the mixture-of-experts, 1.56×. Same hardware, same day, four to seven times the gain.

4Prefill is two terms, and they do not move together

The linear term b is GEMM throughput — how fast the machine multiplies. The quadratic term c is how badly attention scales with prompt length. A prefill tok/s number is a weighted average of the two at one prompt length, and the weighting changes with every prompt. On the dense model the A100's attention advantage is twice its compute advantage — 6.67× against 3.29×. Whatever is limiting the 7900 XT at prefill, it is not mainly the multiplier.

On the mixture-of-experts the two collapse together, 5.71× against 5.75×. The model activates 4B of its 26B parameters per token, so the GEMM term is small on both machines and the ratio between them stops being about the GEMMs at all.

The L4 is slower than a consumer card on b and three times better on c. 0.90× and 3.01×. That is why it is 1.58× faster than a 7900 XT on a 32 K prompt and would lose on a short one: the two curves cross, and which card is "faster at prefill" is a question about prompt length. The second card, for comparison, buys 1.44–1.48× on b and 1.91–2.22× on c — more of the quadratic term than the linear one, which is the same fact the wall-clock numbers above state.

5Two cards against one A100

The lead is what speculation costs the A100. The solid line is the arm nobody is speculating on, and it is flat: the A100 is ahead by 1.30× to 1.46× at every depth, and that is the whole answer to the question this article's title used to ask. Switch MTP on and that lead is gone: 0.88× at 500 tokens, level from 2K through 8K, and only 1.08× at 32K. The Radeons do not get faster. The A100 gives up more by speculating — and not because it drafts badly. Its acceptance is the better of the two, 1.92–2.07 tokens a step against 1.46–1.53. It loses because a speculative step costs it 2.40–2.57× a plain decode step, where the same step costs the Radeons 1.35–1.39×. At batch 1 the pair has compute to spare inside a step; the single card does not, so the fixed price of drafting and verifying four tokens is a much larger fraction of what it was already doing. Both figures are in benchmarks/campaign-2026-08-29/ and benchmarks/cuda-a100/campaign-2026-08-29/.

The U was four points. Turn the probe on and it is there — 1.48× at 1K, down to 1.14× at 16K, back to 1.87× at 32K — and this article was named for it. Eleven matched rungs of the same pair of machines, two rounds a cell, produce a flat 1.30× to 1.46× instead. The 16K point that looked like a minimum is one cell of one run, and its deepest neighbour compares two different depths. The shape did not survive being measured again; the numbers behind it are still on the page because retracting a figure quietly is worse than showing what it said.

6The split is what costs, not the silicon

The 2D attention path — the one speculation falls into on stock vLLM — launches one workgroup per KV head. This model has 16. On one card a rank sees all of them; split across two, each rank sees eight. The starved path is therefore starved twice as hard by the parallelism, and it shows up as retention:

7And it erodes speculation too

The MTP drafter is four layers deep, so the extra forward pass is cheap. What is not cheap is that every draft step is still a step, and a step under TP=2 pays the all-reduce floor whatever the model attached to it costs. The floor does not shrink with the drafter, so the arithmetic that makes speculation worth having is worse on two cards than on one.

9What is not established

Batched throughput, and prefill's constant term

Everything here is batch 1. Batched throughput is a different regime and is not measured at all; where decode stops being bandwidth-bound the second card's 1.1× has no reason to hold, and neither does anything else on this page. Prefill is measured now, but only its two shape terms: the constant a is fitted and never quoted, because on one arm it moved from 9.8 ms to 99.8 ms between two campaigns a month apart and on another it came out negative. A short enough prompt is therefore outside what these fits describe.

gemma-4-31B on one card, and on the A100 at prefill

The 31B has no single-card row and cannot have one on this box — 22 GB of weights, 20 GB of card. It also has no usable A100 prefill: the 2026-08-29 session ran with prefix caching on and 130 of its 132 prefill rungs fail the repeatability cut, and the 2026-08-30 re-run covered only the 12B and the mixture-of-experts. So the 31B appears here in decode only, and the three-step comparison the rest of the article makes is carried by the other two models.

The A100's own realized bandwidth

Figure 6 exists for the Radeons and has no counterpart. The 1.27× reference in Figure 3 is a ratio of datasheet numbers, so a reader should treat it as an upper bound on what the bandwidth difference alone can explain, not as a prediction either side was measured against.

One stack per side, and one card of each kind

Two different vLLM versions, because each side runs the stack it actually uses; two different operating environments, two of them rented Colab VMs whose cross-session variance is unquantified. And there is one A100 and one L4, each measured once: nothing here separates a card from its instance.

Why the second card behaves as it does

That a batch-1 decode step is short enough for a fixed all-reduce to eat most of a second card's bandwidth, and that attention parallelises without one, is consistent with every measurement on this page and is not demonstrated by any of them. No all-reduce was timed here. The three published claims that did try to put a number on that floor were withdrawn on 2026-08-30, because the term they rested on is not measurable from this ladder.
