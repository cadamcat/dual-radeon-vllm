12.1 to 4.2 tok/s fall

A hybrid-SSM model that decodes slower the longer you talk to it

Linear attention is supposed to make long context cheap. On this machine Qwen3.6-27B falls from 12.1 to 4.2 tok/s between 500 and 32 000 tokens, while a dense 8B on the same afternoon keeps 77 % of its short-context rate. The 48 linear-attention layers are not the problem. The 16 that are left are.

2× RX 7900 XT · gfx1100 · TP=2 vLLM 0.23.1.dev1 · ROCm 7.14 VFIO guest · no P2P · PCIe 3.0 cross-die campaign 2026-07-25

Abstract

A hybrid Mamba/gated-delta-net checkpoint loses 65 % of its decode rate between 500 and 32 000 tokens of context under vLLM on RDNA3, a curve fourteen to forty times steeper than any dense model measured on the same machine in the same campaign.

A kernel-level profile puts essentially all of the growth in one place: kernel_paged_attention_2d, the Triton fallback serving the model's 16 full-attention layers, whose per-call cost grows 28.3× while every other kernel in the step is flat or slightly cheaper. The linear-attention layers deliver what the architecture promises.

The fallback is taken because the ROCm custom paged-attention kernel is unreachable for this model three conditions over, and it is slow on this model in particular because 4 KV heads at batch 1 leave the kernel almost nothing to parallelise over. Two independent signals agree: per-card power falls with depth, and normalising by KV bytes read makes the gap wider rather than narrower.

The same checkpoint under llama.cpp on the same machine and the same day holds a slope 28× shallower, which rules out the driver and the hardware. An upstream fix exists and is measured here twice, most recently as a controlled A/B on vLLM 0.27: 9.46× at 32 K. One cell of that A/B is bimodal and is reported as such.

1What was measured, and how

Everything in §2 comes from one campaign: one software stack, one afternoon, every model pinned to it. 292 decode and prefill measurements across five architectures and eleven context lengths. Each cell is served by vllm serve, TP=2, CUDA graphs enabled, --gpu-memory-utilization 0.85, 512-token outputs, and is the mean of two rounds.

Rates are reported with the spread of their own rounds rather than a standard deviation. At two to four rounds per cell a standard deviation is a number with more precision than the sample supports; the range as a fraction of the mean says the same thing without implying a distribution. Every point in Figure 1 carries its round count and that range, visible on hover.

On reading the absolute values This is a VFIO guest with no peer-to-peer between the cards and a cross-die PCIe 3.0 path, chosen deliberately as the least favourable topology. The shapes in this article transfer; the absolute rates are a floor, not a benchmark of the hardware.

2The observation

Five architectures, eleven depths, one campaign. Four of the five decline gently with context in the way a KV cache makes inevitable. The fifth does something else.

The hybrid model is the slowest in absolute terms at every depth, which by parameter count it should not be, and it is also the only one whose curve bends downward instead of flattening. Retention is the cleaner statistic: every other configuration keeps between 67 % and 78 % of its short-context rate at 32 000 tokens. This one keeps 35.1 %.

Expressed as a slope — decode microseconds added per token of context — the campaign gives 4.84 µs for the hybrid model against 0.118 µs for the dense 8B. That is a factor of 41, and it is the number the rest of this article is about.

3Attribution: one kernel, not the architecture

A torch profile at two depths, with CUDA graphs left on and kernel calls attributed to the engine step whose window they fall in, so that chunked-prefill calls are not mixed into the decode figures.

The 48 gated-delta-net layers cost the same at 32 K as at 1 K — 0.95× — which is what linear attention promises and what this implementation delivers. The quantised GEMM is likewise flat. One kernel moves, and it moves by 28.3×.

The arithmetic closes. At 16 calls per step, paged attention goes from 5.71 ms/step to 161.5 ms/step, up 155.8 ms. The decode step itself, measured from the CUDA-graph replay, goes from 85.85 ms to 236.9 ms, up 151 ms. The paged-attention growth accounts for the entire decode slowdown; every other kernel is flat or slightly faster at the longer context.

4Why the fallback is taken

The 16 full-attention layers land on Triton because ROCm's custom paged-attention kernel declines them. The RDNA branch of use_rocm_custom_paged_attention in vllm/platforms/rocm.py requires, among other conditions, head_size == 128 and block_size == 16.

use_rocm_custom_paged_attention · counterfactuals
# measured on the running engine, not read off the config
head_dim                = 256       # gate wants 128
cache_config.block_size = 784       # = 16 x 49, gate wants 16
is_pow2(784)            = False     # fails use_custom on its own path
gqa_ratio               = 6         # this one passes

# forcing either failing condition on its own is still not enough
use_rocm_custom_paged_attention(head_size=128)   -> False
use_rocm_custom_paged_attention(block_size=16)   -> False

No single condition is the blocker. Relaxing either failing condition on its own leaves the gate closed, and chunked_prefill_paged_decode.py fails use_custom on is_pow2 independently of both. The custom kernel is unreachable here three times over, which is why making the fallback fast is the only path that helps this class of model.

The obvious fix does not work Relaxing the head_size check moves the failure rather than removing it. CALL_CUSTOM_LAUNCHER_BLK_HEAD in csrc/rocm/attention.cu dispatches only case 64 and case 128 and TORCH_CHECKs on anything else. The condition is the kernel's actual limit, not conservative gating.

Some of the slope is the kernel, not the architecture. The successor checkpoint, Qwen3.8-27B on vLLM 0.27, was measured on 2026-08-29 against both of the kernels the engine will serve those 16 layers with — no speculation on either arm, same checkpoint, same session, both repeating to better than 0.12 %. Left to itself the ROCm selector takes ROCM_ATTN, because TURBOQUANT is incompatible with the decoder and that is the first fallback it reaches for. Pinned to TRITON_ATTN instead — one flag, no patch — the same ladder is 15.0 % faster at 32K, and retention across the whole span goes from 73.4 % to 84.3 %. Eleven points of the collapse this article is about belong to which kernel serves those layers. The rest does not: even pinned, the model still gives up 15.7 % of its short-context rate by 32K, which is the argument below.

5Why the fallback is slow on this model

Dense models on the same machine take the same Triton path and do not degrade like this. The difference is how much parallelism the kernel has to work with at decode, where the batch is 1.

gemma-4-31B, w4a16 dense16322:10.339
Qwen3.6-27B, hybrid4246:14.840

With 4 KV heads and a batch of 1 there is very little for the kernel to spread across the card's 84 CUs. Two independent signals agree that this is occupancy rather than work:

Power falls as context grows

265 W + 265 W at 518 tokens, 232 W + 227 W at 24 K. No other model in the sweep loses power with depth; the others climb toward 265 W and stay there, and this is the only one that comes back down. The starting values differ — at 518 tokens the 8B is at 245 W, the 12B at 206 W, the 26B MoE at 193 W — so the contrast that matters is the direction, not the level. Idle silicon, not saturated silicon.

Normalising by KV traffic widens the gap

Only 16 of the 64 layers carry a KV cache at all, and the GQA ratio is aggressive, so this model reads the least KV of the three: 64 KiB per token against 144 KiB for Qwen3-8B and 160 KiB for gemma-4-31B. Per KiB read it is 92× the cost of Qwen3-8B and 36× that of gemma-4-31B. It cannot be that this model is simply heavier.

6Control: the same model under a different engine

Same machine, same day, same checkpoint family, only the inference engine changed. If the collapse were in the driver, the GPU, or the topology, it would appear here too.

llama.cpp's ROCm slope lands inside the band the dense models produce under vLLM; its Vulkan slope is below the band, flatter than any of them. Two independent sources therefore agree on what a normal slope looks like on this hardware, but this one configuration sits an order of magnitude outside it.

One disturbed point, stated rather than dropped The ROCm 16 384 point came out at 21.35 tok/s with a standard deviation of 2.635 against 0.06–0.09 everywhere else, and the deeper 24 576 point is faster. That single measurement was disturbed and should not be read on its own. It is kept in the figure because removing inconvenient points quietly is worse than showing one.

Prefill, meanwhile, behaves as the architecture promises: throughput improves with length, 805 → 880 tok/s from 500 to 32 K, while dense models lose 8–44 % over the same range. It is decode that inverts.

7The fix, measured twice

The upstream fix is vllm#45916, which adds a split-KV decode kernel to the Triton fallback: instead of one workgroup per (sequence, KV head) walking the whole context, the KV range is split across more workgroups and reduced afterwards. That is exactly the parallelism this model was short of.

It was verified here first on vLLM 0.23.1 in July — 2.52× at 32 K, single differencing runs at slightly different depths. Then properly, as a controlled A/B on the 0.27 image: both arms on one stack and one harness, one fresh container per cell, only the kernel file swapped, run twice with the arm order reversed.

Pooled across both passes: 1.38× at 1 K, 3.52× at 8 K, 9.46× at 32 K. Decode goes from 261.9 to 27.7 ms per token at 32 K, the slope from 7.408 to 0.262 ms per 1 000 tokens of context, and retention from 1 K to 32 K from 10.2 % to 70.0 %. The collapse is gone.

8What is not established

Four things, because a result that only lists its successes is not a measurement.

One cell of the A/B is bimodal

At 8 192 tokens the patched arm gave 47.02, 40.14, 47.58 and 42.41 tok/s over four passes. Sorted, the largest gap between neighbours is bigger than half the total range and falls in the middle: these are two modes about 15 % apart, not one noisy number. Adding the third and fourth passes made the range wider, 15.8 % to 16.8 %, not narrower. The cause is not the split-count heuristic, which is a pure function of inputs the configuration fixes. No mechanism is established. The 3.52× quoted above is the mean of all four and describes neither cluster.

Figure 4 is a different model on a different stack

Figure 1 is Qwen3.6-27B on 0.23.1; Figure 4 is Qwen3.8-27B on 0.27. On the newer stack this checkpoint also picks a different W4A16 linear kernel, worth roughly 3× on its own and nothing to do with attention. The absolute levels in the two figures are not comparable; the ratios and the shape are.

One machine

Single box, single vendor, batch 1 throughout. The occupancy argument predicts that the collapse should soften as batch size rises, and that is untested here.

What the fix does not fix

Paged attention falls from about 68 % of the decode step to 11 %. The remainder is dominated by the quantised GEMM, roughly 77 % of a decode step at short context. The slope is resolved; the baseline is a different question with a different answer.

9What has changed since

This article is a dated record, not standing advice. Two things have moved since the measurements above.

2026-08-10 · the gate no longer needs widening The July verification widened on_gfx12x() to on_gfx1x() locally so that gfx11 could reach the new kernel. That change was merged into vllm#45916's own branch on 2026-08-10, so the PR now admits RDNA3 as it stands and nothing local is required.
2026-08-28 · the baseline question is answered The July write-up closed on "the slope is resolved; the baseline is a separate question". The 0.27 A/B in Figure 4 answers it: at 32 K the model now decodes at 36.1 tok/s against the 4.2 the original campaign measured, and the residual slope sits inside the band this machine's dense models produce rather than 27 % above it.
2026-08-29 · two numbers in this article did not match its own figure The abstract said the hybrid curve is "four to forty times steeper" than the dense models. Against the band Figure 3 draws, 0.118 to 0.339 µs per token, the hybrid's 4.84 is 14.3× to 41.0× steeper: the floor is fourteen, not four. And "llama.cpp's slope lands inside the band" was true of the ROCm backend and not of the Vulkan one, which at 0.107 is below the band entirely. Both corrected in place, and verify_doc_figures.py now pins the ratio and each backend's position against the band.
